Calorie Maintenance Guide: How to Estimate Your Needs

Maintenance calories are the approximate average energy intake that keeps body weight relatively stable over time. They depend on body size, age, sex, body composition, activity and everyday movement.

How BMR and TDEE fit together

Basal metabolic rate (BMR) estimates the energy used for essential functions at rest. Total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) adds estimated energy from daily movement and exercise. A TDEE calculator is commonly used as a starting estimate for maintenance calories.

How to estimate your maintenance level

  1. Estimate resting needs with a BMR Calculator.
  2. Account for your usual activity using the TDEE Calculator.
  3. Keep your routine reasonably consistent and review average weight trends over several weeks.
  4. If the longer-term trend suggests the estimate is off, make gradual adjustments rather than reacting to one day's reading.

Why scale weight changes day to day

Water balance, food volume and other normal factors can change daily weight. A single weigh-in does not show whether average intake matches energy use.

When maintenance needs change

Needs can shift with body weight, activity, training volume, work routine, sleep and other circumstances. Revisit the estimate when your routine changes substantially.

Maintenance and your goal

Estimated maintenance provides a reference point for planning weight loss or gain, but it is not a medical prescription. Avoid extreme changes. If you are pregnant, under 18, have a medical condition, take medication affected by diet or weight, or have a history of disordered eating, seek individualized professional guidance.

How Many Calories Should I Eat a Day?

There is no single calorie number that fits everyone. Daily energy needs depend on age, sex, height, weight, body composition, activity and individual circumstances. A calculator can provide a useful estimate, not an exact prescription.

1. Estimate your BMR

BMR estimates energy used at rest for essential functions. Common equations use age, sex, height and weight. Start with the BMR Calculator.

2. Account for activity

Walking, work, exercise and everyday movement add to resting energy needs. TDEE combines resting needs with an activity estimate. Use the TDEE Calculator and choose an activity level that reflects your usual routine.

3. Understand your goal

Estimated TDEE can be a starting point for maintenance. Weight loss may involve a moderate reduction, while weight gain may involve an appropriate increase. Individual needs and health circumstances vary.

4. Review longer-term trends

Daily weight can fluctuate because of water, food volume, exercise and other factors. Look at consistent trends over several weeks rather than changing a plan after one day.

What can change calorie needs?

Age, body size, body composition, daily movement, exercise, work patterns and changes in weight can all affect energy needs.

Frequently asked questions

Are calorie calculator results exact?

No. They are estimates and actual energy needs can differ.

Should I use BMR or TDEE?

BMR describes resting energy needs; TDEE estimates total daily expenditure including activity.

Should everyone use the same calorie target?

No. A target should reflect individual needs and circumstances. Seek individualized guidance if you have a medical condition, are pregnant, are under 18 or have specific nutritional needs.

How Much Water Should You Drink a Day?

Fluid needs vary with body size, activity, climate, diet and health. There is no single daily amount that fits everyone.

Why water needs vary

Fluid needs are influenced by body size, age, activity, temperature, humidity, diet and overall health. Someone exercising outdoors in hot weather may need more fluid than someone spending a cool day indoors. Foods such as fruit, vegetables, soups and yogurt also contribute water, so drinking water is not the only source of fluid.

Everyday signs and habits

Thirst is a useful everyday signal for many healthy adults, although it may be less reliable in some older adults and in certain health situations. Keeping water available, drinking with meals and replacing fluids after sweating are practical habits. Urine colour can sometimes offer a rough clue about hydration, but it is affected by foods, supplements and medicines and should not be used alone to assess health.

Exercise and hot weather

During prolonged activity or heat exposure, fluid losses can rise through sweat. Drink regularly and adjust to conditions, while avoiding the temptation to force excessive amounts of water quickly. Long endurance sessions and heavy sweating may require individualized advice about electrolytes as well as fluids.

When to seek individualized advice

People with kidney, heart or liver conditions, or those advised to limit fluids, should follow their clinician's guidance rather than a general online estimate. Seek medical advice for persistent symptoms or concerns about dehydration. Severe confusion, fainting or inability to keep fluids down requires urgent medical attention.

Use estimates as a starting point

Online calculators cannot account for every personal factor and should not override medical advice. Focus on consistent habits and how you feel across ordinary days rather than treating one number as a strict daily requirement.

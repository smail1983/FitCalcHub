How a TDEE and Calorie Calculator Can Help You Understand Your Daily Calorie Needs

Understanding how many calories your body needs each day can be useful when setting realistic health, fitness, or weight-management goals. A TDEE and Calorie Calculator provides an estimate of your daily energy needs based on information such as age, sex, height, weight, and activity level.

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure. It represents an estimate of the amount of energy your body uses throughout an average day. Your TDEE includes the energy your body needs to maintain basic functions, along with calories used through daily movement and physical activity.

A typical TDEE calculation starts with an estimate of your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR). BMR represents the approximate amount of energy your body requires at rest. An activity factor is then used to estimate total daily calorie requirements.

Your estimated calorie needs can change when your body weight, activity level, or daily routine changes. For this reason, a TDEE calculator should be viewed as a starting point rather than an exact measurement.

If your goal is weight maintenance, consuming approximately the number of calories represented by your estimated TDEE may help you maintain your current weight over time. People trying to lose weight may use a moderate calorie deficit, while people trying to gain weight may require additional calories. Individual needs vary, and aggressive calorie restriction may not be appropriate for everyone.

Calories are only one part of a healthy lifestyle. Food quality, protein intake, physical activity, sleep, hydration, and overall dietary habits can also play important roles in health and fitness.

How the TDEE estimate is calculated

This calculator first estimates basal metabolic rate (BMR) with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. With weight in kilograms, height in centimetres and age in years, the equation is:

  • Men: BMR = 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age + 5
  • Women: BMR = 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age − 161

The estimated BMR is multiplied by an activity factor to produce an approximate total daily energy expenditure. The activity factors used here are estimates, not measurements of your actual movement.

Activity levels used by this calculator

  • Sedentary (1.20): mostly sitting with little planned exercise.
  • Lightly active (1.375): light activity or exercise on a few days each week.
  • Moderately active (1.55): moderate exercise or regular movement on most days.
  • Very active (1.725): hard exercise or physically demanding activity on many days.
  • Extra active (1.90): very demanding training or a highly active occupation.

Choose the level that best reflects your usual week, not an unusually active or inactive day. If you are between two levels, compare both estimates and use your longer-term weight and energy trends as context.

Using TDEE for a goal

An estimate near your TDEE may help you understand the energy intake associated with weight maintenance. A planned change in intake can affect body weight over time, but the result is not exact: energy needs adapt, activity varies, and food tracking can be imprecise. Avoid treating a calculator output as a required calorie target.

Frequently asked questions

What does TDEE stand for?

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure. It is an estimate of the energy your body uses in an average day, including basic body functions and daily activity.

How is TDEE calculated?

This calculator first estimates basal metabolic rate (BMR) with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, then multiplies it by an activity factor from 1.20 (sedentary) to 1.90 (extra active).

Is the result exact?

No. The result is an estimate and a starting point. Energy needs vary between people and change over time, so use your longer-term weight and energy trends as context.

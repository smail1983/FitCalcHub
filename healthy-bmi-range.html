Healthy BMI Range: Age, Sex and What the Number Means

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a screening measure calculated from height and weight. For adults, commonly used categories are underweight below 18.5, healthy weight from 18.5 to 24.9, overweight from 25.0 to 29.9, and obesity at 30 or higher. These ranges do not diagnose an individual's health.

Does the healthy BMI range change with adult age?

Standard adult BMI categories are generally used without separate cutoffs for each adult age. Age still matters when interpreting health because muscle mass, body composition, activity and medical circumstances can change over time.

Is there a different BMI range for men and women?

The standard adult screening categories are generally the same for men and women. However, BMI does not directly measure body fat, muscle, bone density or fat distribution, and people with the same BMI can have different body compositions.

Children and teenagers

Adult BMI cutoffs should not be used for children and teenagers. Their BMI is interpreted using age- and sex-specific growth references.

Use BMI in context

Consider waist measurements, body composition, activity, nutrition, medical history and other health information. Use the BMI Calculator as a screening starting point, not a diagnosis. See also the BMI Guide and BMI vs Body Fat.

TDEE Calculator Guide

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure. It is an estimate of how much energy your body uses in an average day, including resting energy needs and physical activity.

How TDEE is estimated

A common calculation starts with an estimate of Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR), then applies an activity factor. The result is a planning estimate rather than a direct measurement of calories burned.

Common activity levels

Activity categories usually range from sedentary to very active. The category should reflect your overall routine, not just a single workout. If your routine changes substantially, your estimated TDEE may change too.

TDEE vs BMR

BMR estimates resting energy needs. TDEE is broader because it attempts to account for daily activity. This is why TDEE is generally more useful when planning a daily calorie target.

How to use a TDEE estimate

For maintenance, the estimate can be used as a starting point for observing whether body weight remains relatively stable over time. For weight loss or muscle gain, people may adjust intake from that starting point while monitoring longer-term trends.

Why your real needs may differ

Actual energy expenditure can vary with body composition, non-exercise movement, training, sleep, health circumstances and changes in body weight. Two people with similar measurements can therefore have different real-world calorie needs.

Common mistakes

  • Treating TDEE as an exact number.
  • Changing calorie targets because of one day's weight change.
  • Choosing an activity level that does not match your usual routine.
  • Making extreme calorie changes based only on a calculator.

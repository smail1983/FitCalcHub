BMI Calculator Guide: How BMI Is Calculated and Its Limits

Last updated: November 17, 2026

What is BMI?

Body mass index (BMI) is a screening measure based on weight relative to height. It can help describe weight status in adults, but it does not directly measure body fat or diagnose a health condition.

How to calculate BMI

For metric measurements, use BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²). With pounds and inches, multiply weight in pounds by 703, then divide by height in inches squared.

For example, a person weighing 70 kg and measuring 1.75 m has a BMI of approximately 22.9.

Common adult BMI screening categories

  • Below 18.5: underweight
  • 18.5 to 24.9: healthy-weight range
  • 25.0 to 29.9: overweight
  • 30.0 or higher: obesity range

These commonly used adult categories are screening ranges, not a diagnosis or a complete definition of an individual's health.

How to use a BMI calculator

  1. Choose the correct unit system (metric or US customary) if the calculator offers that choice.
  2. Enter your current weight and height carefully, using consistent units.
  3. Calculate the result and treat it as an estimate for screening context.
  4. Consider it alongside other information rather than making a health decision from this number alone.

Does BMI change by age, sex or body type?

Standard adult BMI screening categories are generally not divided into separate adult cutoffs for each age or sex. However, age can be relevant to the wider interpretation because muscle mass, body composition, activity and health circumstances may change over time. For children and teenagers, adult BMI categories should not be used; age- and sex-specific growth references are used instead.

What BMI cannot tell you

BMI cannot distinguish muscle from fat, measure bone density, show where body fat is stored, or directly describe fitness, waist circumference, medical history or overall wellbeing. Two people with the same BMI can have different body compositions. A muscular person may have a higher BMI without having the same body-fat profile as someone else with that BMI.

Use BMI as one piece of information

For a broader picture, a healthcare professional may consider waist measurements, body composition, blood pressure, laboratory results, lifestyle and personal medical history. BMI is most useful when interpreted in context and alongside other relevant measures.

Frequently asked questions

Is BMI a diagnosis?

No. It is a screening measure and cannot diagnose an individual health condition.

Is there a different adult BMI range for men and women?

The commonly used adult screening categories are generally the same. Individual interpretation still depends on broader health and body-composition context.

Can athletes have a high BMI?

Yes. BMI does not separate muscle from fat, so a person with substantial muscle mass may have a higher BMI.

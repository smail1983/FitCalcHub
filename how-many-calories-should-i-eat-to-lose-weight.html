How Many Calories Should I Eat to Lose Weight?

There is no single calorie target that works for everyone. A practical starting point is to estimate maintenance needs and then consider a moderate, sustainable calorie deficit.

Start with estimated TDEE

TDEE estimates total daily energy expenditure using factors such as age, height, weight and activity. It is a planning estimate rather than an exact measurement. Try the TDEE Calculator.

Consider a sustainable deficit

A moderate calorie reduction is commonly easier to maintain than extreme restriction. A reduction of a few hundred calories is sometimes used as a planning example, but individual needs and health circumstances vary. The Calorie Deficit Calculator can provide an estimate, not a prescription.

Review progress beyond one weigh-in

Use longer-term changes in body weight, energy, hunger and performance to evaluate whether your plan is appropriate. Daily weight can fluctuate because of water, food volume and other factors.

Choose supportive habits

Focus on nutrient-dense meals, adequate protein and fiber, regular movement and sufficient sleep. Avoid aggressive restriction.

When to get individual advice

If you have a medical condition, are pregnant or breastfeeding, are under 18, take medication affected by diet or weight, or have a history of disordered eating, consult a qualified healthcare professional before changing calorie intake.

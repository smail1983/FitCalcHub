BMR Calculator Guide: How Basal Metabolic Rate Is Estimated

Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) estimates the energy your body uses at rest to support essential functions such as breathing and circulation. It is a starting point for understanding daily energy needs, not your full daily calorie expenditure.

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation

One commonly used adult estimation equation is Mifflin-St Jeor. With weight in kilograms, height in centimeters and age in years:

  • Men: BMR = 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age + 5
  • Women: BMR = 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age − 161

These equations provide estimates; they do not directly measure an individual's metabolism.

BMR, resting metabolic rate and TDEE

Resting Metabolic Rate (RMR) also describes energy used while at rest. BMR and RMR are related concepts, but the measurement conditions are not necessarily identical, so the terms should not automatically be treated as exact synonyms. TDEE is broader: it estimates total daily energy expenditure, including activity.

Why estimates differ

Body composition, activity, health circumstances and other individual factors can affect real energy needs. Use the BMR Calculator as a starting estimate, then use the TDEE Calculator to account for activity.
